️💡️ A discrete-time model of cholera immune period

School of Oceanography, University of Washington

This page explains key mathematical elements of Koelle et al. (2005)'s cholera model.

This model has the the following characteristics:

  • The model structure is a discrete time SIR model with a time unit of 1 month.

  • It assumes the total population in month ttt, NtN_tNt​, is known[1], so that

(1)

  • Koelle et al. assume that a cholera infection lasts less than 1 month, so that all the individuals infected at time ttt are recovered by time t+1t+1t+1.

  • In Equation (1), the recovered population population is determined by:

    • the historical time series of infections

    • the decrease in the immunity level κi\kappa_iκi​ with time since an infection.

  • The immunity level, κi\kappa_iκi​, is the fraction of people retaining an acquired immunity to cholera iii months after they have recovered.

    • The immunity level at t=0t=0t=0 is AAA, the number of asymptomatic (or otherwise unreported) cholera infections for each reported infection.[2]

    • The timescale over which the immunity level declines from AAA to 0 is the immune period, timmunityt_{immunity}timmunity​.

In Equation (3),

  • βt\beta_tβt​ is the transmission rate in month ttt

  • ϵt\epsilon_tϵt​ is a “noise” factor in month ttt

  • α\alphaα and γ\gammaγ are “clustering” exponents, intended to account for the fact that populations are not freely mixing – infected individuals are on average more likely to interact with other infected individuals than would be expected if interactions were completely random.[4]

  • The transmission rate for month ttt is βt=βseasonβclimate\beta_t = \beta_{season} \beta_{climate}βt​=βseason​βclimate​, where

    • βseason\beta_{season}βseason​ is the seasonal transmission component, defined over the 12 months of a year[5]

    • βclimate\beta_{climate}βclimate​ is the climate transmission component, defined over an extended period of several years[6]

    In this implementation, seasonal fluctuations are modulated by the parameter ccc and climate fluctuations are modulated by the parameter bbb.

Footnotes¶

  1. The implementation of this model in this Unit assumes a constant population.

  2. The immune function is the fraction of the actual recovered population that is protected, as a multiplier of the reported number of cases. Because many cases are asymptomatic or otherwise unreported, the reported caseload may drastically underestimate the actual number of cases. The ratio of asymptomatic to symptomatic cases is an unknown parameter that Koelle et al had to estimate as part of their analysis.

    ↩

  3. Koelle et al. include cross-immunity from infection by the Classical biotype; this implementation addresses only the predominant El Tor biotype.

  4. In their implementation, Koelle et al. assume that γ=1\gamma=1γ=1, and use statistical optimization to estimate α\alphaα.

  5. In this implementation, βseason\beta_{season}βseason​ is a periodic function with linearly decreasing amplitude, that is vaguely reminiscent of the seasonal pattern determined by Koelle et al. but mostly chosen for convenience.

  6. In this implementation, βclimate\beta_{climate}βclimate​ linearly increases from the start of a simulation to its midpoint, after which it linearly decreases back to its starting level.
