🧭️ How to use this book

School of Oceanography, University of Washington

This book is organized into Parts, each focused on a biological mechanisms central to a different general topic or biological research area. Currently, work-in=progress Parts are implemented in these topic areas:

  • Biomechanics in physiology, behavior and sensing;

  • Demography, epidemiology and other dynamics of population;

  • Spatial dynamics, in which spatial distributions are essential to model derivations and biological implications;

Future Parts are planned in these topic areas:

  • Experimental methods, including statistics, image/video analysis and other computational tools.

  • Development

  • Genetics

  • Behavior

Each Part contains Units that present, in context, one or more models within the general topic. “In context” means that these Units provide:

  • brief background (with links to additional outside references)

  • one or more Jupyter notebooks with working “executable” models

  • instructions how to use the models, and how to interpret model output

  • activities that explore potential implications of the model for interesting questions in Organismal Biology.

Units are typically constructed from several types of pages, indicated with icons on the Table of Contents:

  • 🧭️ Orientation pages, explaining the book structure and assisting in navigation to topics of interest

  • 🏞️ Introductory pages, with context articulating the essential scientific rationale underlying models and activities

  • 🧮 Worksheet pages, containing Jupyter notebooks with executable models

  • 📖 Background pages, with additional information relevant to model derivation, output interpretation and activities but not essential to making informative use of the Worksheet

  • 👣 Pathway pages, presenting guided activities that illustrate the thought process and modeling strategy behind an example application of the Worksheet

  • 🛼 Activity pages, outlining independent activities -- “mini-studies” -- in which users can use the model to develop and test hypotheses about organism functions and interactions

  • 💡 Key Topic Ideas pages, with more detailed focus on ideas on which models, analysis and activities are based

These different document types overlap in their content, but generally Introductory pages are very useful or necessary for a basic operation of the models, while Background pages contribute to a deeper understanding of models’ foundations, implications and limitations.

Key Topic Ideas are important background that is a somewhat more detailed presentation of a biological or mathematical concept, or a specific idea on which a biological question or modelling approach is founded or interpreted. These pages are intended to be short enough for a reader to internalize the most relevant, essential facts about a topic refered to in a model, without losing continuity of thought while working with that model. Key Topic Ideas are not intended to be comprehensive, though ideally they may refer to additional sources that provide in-depth perspectives. In many cases the most useful reference is simply a link to Wikipedia, which has proven to usually be comprehensive and accurate in many areas of biology, mathematics, physics, statistics and other disciplines.

The Appendix contains useful information relevant to the ebook as a whole:

  • A list of links to Binder that launches each of the Jupyter Notebooks in this ebook

  • A search tool for an index of available Units and models, implemented as a Jupyter Notebook (usable through Binder) that enables users to select ebook pages according to document type (Model, Context, Activity, etc.), Topic (Biomechanics, Demography, etc.), relevant levels of biological organization (cell, organism, habitat, ecosystem, etc.), or key mechanisms (developmental, demographic, genetic, etc.)

  • A set of “Math thumbnails”, that are Key Topic Pages summarizing key mathematical concepts in a clear and concise format

Usage¶

In general, users are expected to focus on a single Unit at a time, optionally followed by other related Units at linked organizational levels, or at larger or smaller spatial and temporal scales. For example, a user who investigates movement of particles and embryos through fluids might apply their findings to an investigation of larval retension in estuaries, to understand the population and geographical implications of biomechanical characteristics, or to the size distributions of diatoms to understand how sinking out of the mixed layer might affect aquatic photosynthesizers. A user investigating the demographic implications of cloning in marine invertebrate larvae might interpret their demographic conclusions in the context of size/shape effects on larval swimming, assess variation of fertilization and polyspermy rates as functions of turbulence and size-dependent sinking rates transport of particles in currents, or identify physiological factors affected by limits to diffusive exchange for moving particles.

The intended method for most readers to use the book is to:

  • read the “Introductory” pages in the GitHub Pages e-book

  • after selecting a Unit, use the “button” links to open and execute the models in the worksheet notebook on Binder

  • after getting familiar with the model, follow the Pathway guided activity to learn some strategies for using the model to gain biological insight

  • choose one of the suggested independent Activities (or make up one of your own), and address the motivating questions by designing and executing systematic data collection. analysis and interpretation

  • refer to the Unit’s “Background” and “Key Topic Ideas” (and their cited references) where helpful for additional information

In courses and some other settings, an opportunity may be present to

  • write a brief narrative, or make a slide or two, that presents an argument, conclusion or analysis using graphical or textual output from a model

Static vs. executable book content¶

It’s important for ebook users to understand the differences between static and executable book content:

  • Executable book content is that which is accessed through Binder, consisting of Worksheets implemented in Jupyter notebooks, sometimes with supporting Python code.
    These notebooks provide interactive interfaces for setting parameters, running models and viewing output, for experiential learning by users.

  • Static book contents are those accessed through GitHub Pages.
    These contents include all the types of pages: Orientation, Introductory, Background, Activity and Key Topic Area pages. They also include Worksheets, but only as static non-operable copies. These copies cannot be executed like the ones on Binder can, but they provide clickable links to the corresponding executable Worksheets on Binder.
