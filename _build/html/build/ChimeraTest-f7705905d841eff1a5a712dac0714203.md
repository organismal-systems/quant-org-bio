# 🛼 Testing hypothetical performance standards: Parameter variation
The range of early-stage larval morphologies that can be generated by the model is quite broad, and includes a large parameter space.
However, to explore a hypothetical performance standard for upswimming speed feasible within the constraints of an Activity, this parameter space must be condensed and prioritized.

In this Activity, we accomplish this by first switching from the geometrical parameter set in [](./ChimeraDesign.ipynb) to the organism-based parameter set in [](./ChimeraOrg.ipynb), and then restricting variation to a subset of organism-based parameters.

## Organism-based *vs.* geometrical parameters
The motivation for defining two sets of parameters is that they define larval morphologies in ways that lend themselves to two different purposes:
1. Organism-based parameters are intuitive to use in posing biological hypotheses, because they are expressed in terms of organismal constraints such as the tissue volume, and measurable functional traits such as excess density.
2. Geometrical parameters are necessary for constructing elements of the biomechanical model, but are unintuitive for posing biological hypotheses because they confound variations in multiple organismal traits.

Specifying either of these parameter sets also specifies the other.
Because the translations between them are not straightforward, they are implemeted in code below.

### Geometrical parameters
Larval morphologies in the [ChimeraSwim](./ChimeraSwim.ipynb) are defined by the geometrical parameters shown in this diagram:

![alt text](ChimeraSpheroid_geometry2.png "Surface shape parameters")

The parameters defining the external shape of the larva are:
- $D_s$: the maximum diameter of the external surface, at its equator
- $L_{s_1}$: the length of the top semi-spheroid (distance from the upper tip to the equator) of the external surface
- $L_{s_2}$: the length of the bottom semi-spheroid (distance from the lower tip to the equator) of the external surface

The parameters defining the shape of the internal "inclusion" are:
- $D_i$: the maximum diameter of the inclusion, at its equator
- $L_{i_1}$: the length of the top semi-spheroid (distance from the upper tip to the equator) of the inclusion
- $L_{i_2}$: the length of the bottom semi-spheroid (distance from the lower tip to the equator) of the inclusion
- $h_i$: the height of the inclusion equator above the external shape equator

It's assumed that the ambient seawater density is $1030 \frac{kg}{m^3}$.

### Organism-based parameters





To this end, we will consider one test of the "performance metric" hypothesis to comprise *measurement of upswimming velocity, across systematic variation in larval tissue mass and excess density*.
Within that one test, *the other parameters that determine shape and material properties are to be held constant*.
This can be followed, of course, with additional tests using other shapes and material properties, to explore the generality of the results of the first test.






### Fixed parameters
[](#parsF) shows the parameters to be fixed within a series of observations of model results constituting one hypothesis test.
That is, in [](./ChimeraOrg.ipynb), you are to choose a value for each of these parameters for a complete series of simulations (varying "focal" parameters) during one test, returning later to change one or more of them for additional tests.
:::{table} Table of fixed parameters. The relevant ranges shown are only indicative; some interesting examples may lie outside these ranges.
:align: center
:label: parsF
| Parameter | Symbol | Relevant range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aspect ratio | $\alpha$ | 0.5-2 |
| Relative equator position | $\eta$ | 0.2-0.8 |
| Relative inclusion position | $\sigma$ | 0.75-0.99 |
| Tissue density | $\rho_{tissue}$ | 1070-1300 $\frac{kg}{m^3}$ |
| Inclusion density | $\rho_{inclusion}$ | 900-2669 $\frac{kg}{m^3}$ |
:::
- The aspect ratio, $\alpha$, is a shape parameter determining the height of the larva relative to its diameter.
- The relative equator position, $\eta$, determines how high is the widest part of the larva.
- The relative inclusion position, $\sigma$, is the height of the inclusion within the external surface.
- The densities $\rho_{tissue}$ and $\rho_{inclusion}$ are material properties of the tissue and inclusion.  

  The extremes of the relevant range represent lipid and calcite.
  The simplest and most constraining assumption may be that the inclusion contains seawater ($\rho_{seawater}=1030 \frac{kg}{m^3}$) or freshwater ($\rho_{freshwater}=1000 \frac{kg}{m^3}$).

#### Constraints on the relative inclusion position
The relative inclusion position parameter, $\sigma$, uses a simple formula to position the inclusion within the external surface.

**This parameter has two important constraints**:
- It cannot exceed $\sigma<1$
- It must be sufficiently close 1 that the inclusion lies entirely within the external surface of the larva (this threshold differs across choices of other parameters).

The suggested strategy in this Activity is to leave this parameter at the default, $\sigma = 0.99$, at least initially.
With this choice, nearly all larval forms generated with the formula contain the inclusion entirely within the external surface.
However, it is possible to explore other values of $\sigma$, as long as you check to make sure the above constraints are satisfied.

### Focal variable parameters
In the Activity, one test of the hypothesis consists of a systematic survey through the parameters in [](#parsV).
:::{table} Table of systematically varied parameters. The relevant ranges shown are only indicative; some interesting examples may lie outside these ranges.
:align: center
:label: parsV
| Parameter | Symbol | Relevant range |
| --- | --- | --- |
|Tissue volume | $V_t$ | $10^{-14}-10^{-11} ~m^3$ |
| Average excess density | $\rho_{excess}$ | $20-300 \frac{kg}{m^3}$ |
:::

- Tissue mass is a metric of larval size that relates directly to trade-offs in egg size and other physiological limitations.
Average excess density (combined with the fixed parameters) is a relatively easily measured larval characteristic[^dens].
- In [](./ChimeraOrg.ipynb), this functions effectively as a specification of inclusion size.
That is, the model "inflates" the external surface of the larva by expanding it isometrically, as much as necessary to achieve the specified average excess density.

[^dens]: Two relatively straighforward experimental techniques for this are: (a) measuring sinking rates in a stationary water column and [calculating the implied body force](../ReSphere/RS1.ipynb), or (b) equilibrium positions of larvae in a water column with a density gradient. In both cases, ciliary action is chemically suppressed.

For example, suppose a larva has tissue density of  $\rho_{tissue} = 1230 \frac{kg}{m^3}$ and an inclusion filled with seawater,  $\rho_{inclusion} = 1030 \frac{kg}{m^3}$.
If this larva has an average excess density $\rho_{excess}=100 \frac{kg}{m^3}$, then its actual average density is $\rho_{excess}+\rho_{seawater}=1130 \frac{kg}{m^3}$.
Because this larva has a density midway between $\rho_{tissue}$ and $\rho_{inclusion}$, it must comprise 50% tissue volume and 50% inclusion volume, so its total enclosed volume would be $2 V_t$.

In this example, a lower $\rho_{excess}$ implies a larger fraction of internal volume is inclusion, so its volume is larger.
Conversely, a higher $\rho_{excess}$ implies a smaller fraction of internal volume is inclusion, so its volume is smaller.

### Additional implicit parameters
To reduce the parameter space, it is assumed that:
- The shape of the inclusion is the same as the shape of the external surface.
- The ciliary speed (that is, the water velocity generated by cilia at the larval surface) is $V_{cilia} = 1000 \frac{\mu m}{s}$.
- Larvae are *axisymmetric*, meaning that the inclusion is centered on the same vertical axis as the external surface.

Each of these "implicit" choices excludes alternatives that would likely have different swimming performance.

The choices of focal, fixed and implicit assumptions are to some extent arbitrary.
These choices essentially reflect selection of parameters hypothesized to have the largest fundamental effects on performance as focal parameters.
If this hypothesis is correct, then the implications of variation in the characteristics are most efficiently explored *after* some of the fundamental consequences of variations in size and excess density have been elucidated.
