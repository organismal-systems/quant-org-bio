# 🏞️ Biotic and abiotic constraints on egg size and early development in marine invertebrate larvae
Organisms are limited by their metabolic resources, and among the most consequential ways in which resource limitations are expressed are constraints on reproduction.
Among most marine invertebrates, reproduction occurs *episodically*: 
Gametes (or their precursors) are accumulated over a prolonged period of months or even years.
This is followed, in most cases, by a [spawning](wiki:Spawning) event in which gametes are released into the water for fertilization.
One constraint on spawning is that individuals (in some cases, whole populations) precisely synchronize their release of gametes  so that eggs and sperm encounter each before they are dispersed by currents and turbulence. 
After fertilization, development through a species-specific sequence of embroyonic and larval stages follows during a planktonic phase.
The planktonic phase ends when the larvae achieve *competence*, meaning they have arrived at a state of physiological development that the appropriate environmental cues can stimulate them to metamorphose.
