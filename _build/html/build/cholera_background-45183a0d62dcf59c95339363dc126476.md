# 🏞️ Environmental and physiological factors in cholera outbreaks



Background information on cholera
The CDC estimates there are 3-5 million cholera cases, causing over 100,000 deaths, each year around the world. Cholera is caused by a bacterium, Vibrio cholerae. Cholera is a difficult disease to contain.  Reasons for this include:
    • The period of immunity – how long a person who has recovered from cholera has increased resistance to being reinfected – is unknown.
    • Many (probably most) cases of cholera are asymptomatic or have only mild symptoms. Even when surveillance data from clinics are available (which they often aren't) these cases are typically not counted. Hence, the true incidence of cholera is rarely known.  
    • Exposure is affected by environmental conditions such as rainfall, flooding, etc., which are highly variable across seasons and subject to climatic variability.
    • People are exposed to cholera bacteria through multiple transmission pathways, including exposure to bacteria occurring naturally in estuaries and other natural environments and exposure to bacteria in human feces. Environmental variations that suppress one transmission mechanism might promote another, either immediately or after a delay.
    • Cholera is caused by several distinct bacterial strains, that differ in their biology (e.g., preferred natural habitats) and in their impacts on human hosts (e.g., severity of symptoms, immunity across strains, etc.). 
Because cholera is affected simultaneously by all these factors, it is difficult to establish the roles of any one of them. Consequently, it's unclear which of the many possible alternative strategies to reduce or mitigate cholera is the best use of resources.

Koelle et al. (2005) developed a model in which they tried to simultaneously quantify several epidemiological and environmental factors affecting cholera incidence in a focal site, Matlab, Bangladesh. Their paper was a huge parameter-fitting exercise, which is quite complicated, but the model they used to simulate cholera outbreaks is relatively simple. In these Study Questions, we will use a recreation of their model in the worksheet cholera4.sws to gain insights into the effectiveness of possible cholera mitigation strategies.
