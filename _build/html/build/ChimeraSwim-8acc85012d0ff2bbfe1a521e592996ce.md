# 🏞️ Biotic and abiotic constraints on egg size and early development in marine invertebrate larvae
Organisms are limited by their metabolic resources, and among the most important ways in which resource limitations are expressed are constraints on reproduction.
Because reproductive constraints are impacted by a wide variety of organismal and environmental traits, and because they have such sweeping consequences for the size, stability and range of populations, the evolutionary and developmental context of the embryos and larvae through which invertebrate reproduction is accomplished are a major focus of [larval ecology](wiki:Marine_larval_ecology).

In a typical marine invertebrate [life history](wiki:Life_history_theory), reproduction occurs *episodically*: 
Gametes (or their precursors) are accumulated over a prolonged period of months or even years.
This is followed, in most cases, by a [spawning](wiki:Spawning) event in which gametes are released into the water for fertilization.[^spw]
Fertilization is followed by development through a species-specific sequence of embroyonic and larval stages, usually during a planktonic phase.[^plk]
The planktonic phase ends when the larvae achieve *competence*, meaning they have arrived at a state of physiological development that the appropriate environmental cues can stimulate them to metamorphose. 
These cues indicate a suitable habitat for a juvenile to survive and mature into a reproductive adult.






[^spw]: One constraint on spawning is that individuals (in some cases, whole populations) precisely synchronize their release of gametes  so that eggs and sperm encounter each before they are dispersed by currents and turbulence. Many taxa synchronize using environmental cues such as phases of the moon or storms, as well as chemical cue from conspecifics.
[^plk]: In some cases, fertilization happens internally. In many such cases, larvae are also planktonic, but some taxa undergo development within egg capsules or are brooded inside their mother.
