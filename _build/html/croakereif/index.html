💡️ Effects of changes in egg size in the croaker model

School of Oceanography, University of Washington

The speculative addition of egg size evolution to Diamond et al. (2000)'s Atlantic croaker population model assumes that the allocation to each egg is under evolutionary control, and that increases or decreases in egg size are reflected in different amounts of time spent in the ocean larva stage. The effect of changes in egg size are implemented in three inter-related parameters:

  • the Egg Investment Factor, EIFEIFEIF, which is the factor of increase or decrease of resources invested in each egg, relative to the current investment.

  • the Duration Factor, DFDFDF, which is the factor of increase or decrease of the duration of the Ocean Larva stage during the first year of croaker development .

  • The growth rate, α\alphaα, during the Ocean Larva stage.

Larval growth rates¶

The final step in this analysis is to gain perspective on plausible ranges for the growth rate parameter, α\alphaα.

To best reflect constraints on maternal resources available for reproduction, we have used mass as our currency of larval size. We do not currently have measured rates of increase for larval mass during the Ocean Larva stage.

However, Diamond et al. (1999)) calculated growth rates for these larvae for the Atlantic and Gulf populations, expressed in terms of average length increase per day.
